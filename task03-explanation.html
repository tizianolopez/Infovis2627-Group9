Gestalt Layout & Organization

We structured Alicia Ferrer's road-safety dashboard using Gestalt principles to ensure immediate visual parsing and minimize cognitive friction. Following the law of Common Region (Enclosure), cards group interrelated metrics inside crisp rectangular containers with subtle borders. Proximity groups the six analytical questions into three functional thematic modules: temporal dynamics (evolution line chart and monthly deviation bars on the left), spatial concentration (geographic symbol map and street lollipop ranking in the center), and behavioral factors (hour-by-weekday heat map and district parallel columns on the right). Global filters across the top bar establish visual Continuity across the entire interface.

Visual Encoding & Perception Theory

Visual encodings are strictly grounded in Cleveland and McGill's hierarchy of perceptual tasks. Position along a common scale is prioritized for temporal trends (line chart) and street volume ranking (lollipop chart), enabling rapid, highly accurate quantitative comparisons. Geographic coordinates encode spatial distribution on the symbol map, with bubble area indicating local accident volume. For the temporal correlation heat map, a single-hue sequential lightness scale represents collision intensity without introducing false categorical boundaries, supporting pre-attentive detection of peak rush-hour windows.

Color Strategy & Decision Support

The color system follows a deliberate semantic hierarchy: neutral slates and grays represent baseline collision volumes, while a high-salience crimson alert hue (#DC2626) is reserved exclusively for severe injuries and fatalities. This visual pop-out allows Alicia to swiftly isolate life-threatening corridors during municipal council briefings without cognitive overload.
